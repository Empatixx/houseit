## Floors

Every room takes a floor material:

```bash
update-room --room kitchen --material tile-slate
```

Floors include oak, ash, beech and birch boards; white, beige, blue, slate, hexagonal, herringbone and terracotta tiles; marble, limestone, granite, soapstone and terrazzo; concrete and brick; and outdoor surfaces such as grass, gravel, pavers, asphalt and water. All of them tile seamlessly and are laid at their real size, so a 300 mm tile is 300 mm in any room and boards run on across a doorway.

## Walls, ceilings, doors and windows

```bash
update-room --room bedroom --walls white --ceiling white --doors oak-light --windows black
update-room --room bedroom --walls '#d8cbb5'
```

Each part takes a named finish or a colour of your own as `#rrggbb`.

## Styles

`--style` applies a coordinated set of finishes: `scandinavian`, `coastal`, `farmhouse`, `industrial`, `midcentury-modern`, `traditional`, `rustic` or `modern`.

## Surfaces on furniture

A symbol and a surface are separate. The symbol's white becomes the surface colour and the lines stay lines. One bed is white, linen or blue without there being three beds.

```bash
add-object --room bedroom --type queen-bed --against north --surface linen
```

## Exterior walls

`--exterior` describes the layers of an outside wall and the colour of its face, optionally in bands. See [room commands](/docs/commands/room).
